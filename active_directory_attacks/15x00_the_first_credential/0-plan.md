# Initial Engagement Plan — Vanmark Group (`vanmark.local`)

To obtain an initial credential from a zero-knowledge network position without generating premature noise, I shall begin with passive observation of the local broadcast segment prior to initiating active traffic. Windows hosts routinely emit unsolicited name-resolution traffic upon DNS failure alongside associated authentication exchanges. My sequence of operations is structured as follows: first, passive listening and capture of spontaneously transmitted authentication material; second, should passive observation yield insufficient or no usable material within a defined observation window, transition to a measured, targeted secondary approach rather than an indiscriminate host or subnet scan. I shall abandon the initial passive approach once the observation window ceases to produce actionable material or a more viable, low-noise secondary vector becomes evident.

Captured authentication material shall not be subjected to uniform cracking expenditure. Prior to committing compute resources, I shall evaluate each artifact according to cryptographic strength and crackability within the available timeframe, account relevance and operational utility once recovered, and alignment with the engagement objective. An indiscriminate "crack everything" strategy squanders time and compute capacity on low-value or computationally prohibitive targets.

The binding constraint governing my conduct against Vanmark Group accounts is zero account lockouts. I shall not execute password spraying or repeated authentication guesses without first verifying the domain's actual lockout policy—specifically the failure threshold, observation window, and lockout duration—rather than relying on assumed defaults. Should I be unable to establish this policy reliably, I shall refrain from repetitive authentication attempts or spraying entirely to prevent locking any account, particularly within the finance department.

Prior to any deliberate offensive action, I expect the domain controller to record telemetry attributable to network attachment and baseline host activity, including lease or interface events, name-resolution or network-discovery traffic, and any failed or successful authentication events generated once traffic reaches domain services.
