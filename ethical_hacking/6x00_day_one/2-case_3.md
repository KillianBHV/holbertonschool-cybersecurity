# CASE 3: The Overreach

## 1. What Happened (Factual Summary)
Apex Security Partners signed a **“Red Team assessment”** contract with InnovateTech based on a misunderstanding of the CEO's expectations; in reality, he only wanted **a web and phishing audit**. The Apex team interpreted the term according to its strict technical definition and attempted a physical intrusion at the company's headquarters. A consultant managed to gain access to the server room by posing as a delivery person, before being **confronted by security and arrested by the police**. Although the charges were dropped, news of the incident leaked to the media, destroying Apex’s reputation and causing *the loss of several major clients.*

## 2. What rule(s), law(s), or professional standard(s) were violated
* **Trespass and Breaking and Entering Law:** Covert and unauthorized entry into secure private premises without the explicit consent of management. 
* **Professional Red Teaming Standards (NIST / CREST):** A flagrant violation of client expectation management. A Red Team exercise is not permitted to include physical actions unless the geographic locations and procedures have been explicitly detailed and individually approved. 
* **Ethics and Professional Conduct:** Lack of transparency during the pre-sales phase, as the sales engineer knowingly disregarded the client’s lack of cybersecurity maturity in order to sell them an unsuitable service.

## 3. What the correct professional behavior would have been
Apex’s sales engineer and technical director should have conducted in-depth scoping workshops to educate the CEO on the difference between a penetration test (technical/web-based) and a Red Team simulation (which involves human and physical elements). They should have refused to include the term “Red Team” in the contract without attaching specific scenarios. For his part, before forcing open a door, the field consultant should have verified that he had a valid, signed, physical work order in his pocket.

## 4. What specific clause(s) in a pre-engagement document would have prevented this outcome
* **Physical Security Testing Addendum:** A mandatory addendum detailing the exact list of target physical addresses, authorized intrusion times, and physical bypass methods approved by management.
* **Official Authorization Letter (“Get Out of Jail Free” Letter):** An official physical assessment authorization document, signed by the CEO, which the auditor must carry on their person to present immediately to law enforcement or security personnel in the event of capture. 
* **Attack Objectives and Vectors Definition Clause (Attack Vectors White-List):** A clause explicitly listing authorized attack vectors (e.g., Web and Email only) and formally excluding others (e.g., proximity, physical, Wi-Fi).
