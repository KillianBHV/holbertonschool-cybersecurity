# Engagement Plan

## 1. Hash Cracking Triage Strategy
To optimize time and compute resources during this assessment, we will abandon the inefficient "crack everything" approach. Recoverable material (such as Kerberoastable or AS-REP roastable tickets) will be carefully triaged before committing brute-force hours. We will prioritize service accounts that have high privileges, such as those with write or delegation rights over critical domain objects, while cross-referencing their password last-set dates and structural patterns. High-entropy, machine-generated, or long-character passwords will be discarded early during triage to prevent wasting critical engagement hours on uncrackable hashes.
## 2. Utilizing Recovered Material and Pass-the-Hash Techniques
We intend to leverage the fundamental mechanics of the Kerberos protocol to move laterally and vertically. For accounts where a plaintext password is never obtained, we will utilize Pass-the-Hash (PtH) or Pass-the-Ticket (PtT) techniques. Kerberos architecture permits authentication using valid cryptographic material directly; knowing the actual plaintext character string is completely unnecessary. Furthermore, we will actively look to recognize and chain delegation abuses (such as unconstrained or constrained delegation). This allows us to impersonate target administrative identities and access Thornbury’s actuarial database server without ever needing to recover or crack an administrator's actual password.
## 3. Domain Controller Logs and Engagement Constraints
From the absolute beginning of this engagement, before any active exploitation occurs, we expect the Domain Controller to record standard security event logs. Specifically, it will log every Kerberos ticket request (TGT and TGS requests, such as Event IDs 4768 and 4769) generated by our low-privilege foothold. The client’s SOC will reconstruct our engagement from these exact artifacts. To maintain a safe and controlled assessment, we establish a strict operational boundary: we will not touch or target the Domain Controller itself. Additionally, we will not look for local exploits on the database server. This engagement is treated strictly as an identity and authentication problem, not a system exploitation task.
